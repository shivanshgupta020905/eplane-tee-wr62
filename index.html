Overview

An air-filled E-Plane Tee junction was designed and simulated for the WR-62 rectangular waveguide using Ansys HFSS. The junction is a three-port waveguide component where the auxiliary arm lies in the plane of the dominant TE₁₀ electric field. This project demonstrates its behaviour as an equal power divider and verifies the characteristic 180° phase opposition between output ports.

Why 180° Phase Difference?

When a signal enters Port 3 (E-arm), it splits at the T-junction into two waves travelling in opposite directions along the main guide. The transverse E-field (Eᵧ) must satisfy boundary conditions at the junction — this forces the field to reverse direction for one of the output arms, producing a 180° phase opposition.

↑ Snapshot at 15 GHz, phase = 60°. Both ports carry equal power but with opposite E-field orientations — a 180° phase difference confirmed by vector field plots.

  • E-field vectors at Port 1 point upward (+Y)
  • E-field vectors at Port 2 point downward (−Y) simultaneously
  • This is the geometric antisymmetry of the E-plane junction
  • H-plane Tee outputs are in-phase — the key distinguishing property
